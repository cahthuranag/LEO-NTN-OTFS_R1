\begin{abstract}
Low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite constellations are an enabling technology for non-terrestrial networks (NTNs) in sixth-generation (6G) systems. However, achieving extreme reliability over NTN links for mission-critical applications remains challenging, particularly for high-mobility terminals. Conventional retransmission schemes such as automatic repeat request (ARQ) and hybrid-ARQ (HARQ) are incompatible with stringent latency constraints. This paper proposes a diversity-transform architecture to enhance the reliability of orthogonal time--frequency space (OTFS)-modulated LEO NTN downlinks. Existing transmission diversity techniques based on selection combining or maximal-ratio combining (MRC) provide no guaranteed recovery under simultaneous channel outages. The proposed approach integrates error-correction coding with transmission diversity to achieve extreme reliability without retransmissions. The diversity transform is inserted between the forward error-correction (FEC) encoder and the physical subchannels, allowing integration with various coding schemes and channel models. In the diversity transform, the data word is divided into sub-data words, and each sub-data word is encoded using FEC. The diversity mapper then spreads these sub-data words across available subchannels using a transform matrix based on maximum distance separable (MDS) codes. This paper proposes a transform comprising a baseline fixed transform matrix and an adaptive transform matrix. The baseline constructs a binary diversity matrix derived from a Reed--Solomon code, guaranteeing full-rank recovery whenever a sufficient number of satellite subchannels survive. The adaptive transform extends the baseline by leveraging predicted subchannel reliability scores to select the optimal transform while preserving the recovery guarantees given by the baseline matrix. Reliability scores are obtained from a finite-blocklength error probability analysis derived for OTFS transmission. Simulation results confirm that the proposed architecture substantially  reduces BLER over standalone FEC schemes without retransmissions, with the  adaptive transform outperforming the fixed baseline across all evaluated 
conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{W}{ith} over 2.2 billion people remaining offline 
globally, equivalent to more than one quarter of the world's population 
lacking Internet access~\cite{ITU2025Facts}, low-Earth-orbit (LEO) satellite 
constellations have emerged as the most credible path to truly global 
connectivity~\cite{Kodheli2021,3GPP38821,Lin2021,Luo24}. Yet as LEO networks transition from  broadband internet to mission-critical applications such as autonomous vehicles, 
remote surgery, and industrial control, the tolerance for link outages shrinks  from seconds to milliseconds \cite{Azari2022, Nguyen2024} . Achieving extreme reliability over LEO NTN links 
therefore remains an open problem, particularly for applications that demand  stringent error-rate and latency guarantees~\cite{Nguyen2024, Durisi2016,3GPP_TR38811}. 
These challenges are exacerbated when serving high-mobility terminals such as  high-speed trains, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and vehicular platforms,  where rapid channel variations compound the already severe Doppler shifts 
induced by LEO orbital motion~\cite{Li2021}. Conventional retransmission 
schemes such as automatic repeat request (ARQ) and hybrid-ARQ (HARQ) are 
incompatible with these latency constraints, motivating a coding framework that 
jointly employs error correction and transmission diversity to achieve extreme 
reliability without retransmissions.

Satellite diversity, whereby a user equipment (UE) connects simultaneously 
to multiple visible LEO satellites, is a well-established countermeasure 
against shadowing and blockage~\cite{10268002,596901,Buzzi2023,11299592} 
without employing retransmission schemes. The resulting channel, comprising 
multiple independently fading subchannels, is inherently capable of 
supporting transmission diversity. However, current multi-connectivity 
designs combine independently received signal replicas through selection 
or maximal-ratio combining (MRC)~\cite{li2024multiple}. These approaches 
offer statistical diversity gains but cannot guarantee data recovery when 
several subchannels are simultaneously lost, a scenario that frequently 
arises due to the correlated elevation-angle dynamics of co-visible LEO 
satellites at low elevations. The diversity transform framework moves beyond 
probabilistic combining techniques, such as selection or maximal-ratio 
combining, to provide deterministic outage protection in multi-subchannel 
environments. A diversity transform inserts a structured linear mixing layer, 
represented by a rectangular binary matrix, between the forward 
error-correction (FEC) encoder output and the physical subchannels. The 
encoder maps a data word into multiple output subwords, and the diversity 
mapper spreads these subwords across the available subchannels according to 
the transform matrix. A key architectural feature is the modular separation 
between error correction and diversity functions, providing well-defined 
interfaces toward the encoder and toward the channel. This allows a given 
diversity transform to be composed with any FEC scheme, including 
low-density parity-check (LDPC), turbo, or polar codes, and any compatible 
channel model without requiring cross-layer redesign. The algebraic strength 
of this framework lies in its full-rank criterion. When a subchannel outage 
erases an entire group of transform outputs, the data word remains recoverable 
if and only if the surviving submatrix retains full row rank. Unlike 
statistical combining methods, which degrade under correlated outages, the 
full-rank criterion delivers a certified recovery guarantee: whenever the 
realised outage pattern belongs to the design family, perfect recovery is 
assured regardless of the fading realisations on surviving subchannels.

While the diversity transform addresses inter-satellite outage protection, 
the physical-layer modulation on each subchannel must contend with the severe 
Doppler shifts and multipath induced by rapid LEO orbital motion, which are 
further compounded by UE mobility. The resulting doubly selective channel 
renders conventional orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) 
unsuitable owing to inter-carrier interference from large Doppler 
spreads~\cite{Li2021}. Orthogonal time--frequency space (OTFS) modulation 
addresses this limitation by mapping symbols onto a delay--Doppler grid and 
exploiting the quasi-static channel structure to achieve full multipath 
diversity~\cite{Hadani2017,Raviteja2018}. Existing OTFS studies for LEO 
satellite communications~\cite{Shi2022} have confirmed the robustness of 
OTFS under high mobility; however, no prior work derives finite-blocklength 
error expressions for an OTFS-based satellite communication system.
\subsection{Related Works}
\label{sec:related_works}

Achieving reliable data recovery over independently fading subchannels has been  a long-standing objective. Signal space diversity through rotated lattice  constellations~\cite{Boutros2010} harvests diversity by spreading information  across subcarriers without bandwidth expansion; however, it assumes independent  symbol-level fading and does not address the block-erasure structure of multi-satellite outages, where the loss of a single satellite erases an entire group of resource elements. Moreover, packet-level erasure coding using Reed--Solomon  codes has been applied to satellite links~\cite{10639715}; however, it operates
above the physical layer and assumes successful decoding on each individual
link. On the coding side, polar codes have been combined with interleaving and multi-kernel constructions to provide resilience over block-fading
channels~\cite{Ju2022,Niu2021,Mori2010}, yet none of these methods is underpinned by an algebraic recovery criterion, and none provides certified worst-case guarantees under structured outage patterns. In contrast, the diversity transform framework in ~\cite{Arikan2025_patent} 
provides provable full-rank recovery whenever a sufficient number of subchannels 
survive block-aligned erasures, without requiring interleavers. However, it is 
presented as a general algebraic framework without application to any specific 
channel or communication system. This paper 
addresses this gap by instantiating the diversity transform framework in a 
practical LEO NTN communication system.
From a waveform perspective, OTFS has emerged as a promising waveform for LEO satellite links owing to its ability to exploit the inherent channel sparsity in the delay--Doppler domain~\cite{Shi2022}. Coded OTFS was shown
in~\cite{Li2021} to achieve full multipath diversity when the frame dimensions  exceed the resolvable path count, and it was shown in~\cite{Shi2024_Network}
that OTFS converts the doubly selective channel to an approximately
time-invariant delay--Doppler representation. Despite these advances, all
existing OTFS studies for LEO satellites focus on physical-layer performance
in isolation and do not develop the cross-layer reliability architecture needed for stringent BLER targets.

Finite-blocklength analysis has received considerable attention in the literature, driven by the short-packet transmission requirements of mission-critical communications. Non-asymptotic bounds on achievable rate at finite blocklength were established for short-packet communication in~\cite{Polyanskiy2010}, with extensions to quasi-static fading channels in~\cite{Yang2014,Durisi2016}. In LEO NTN systems, however, short-packet transmission is further constrained by the limited OTFS frame dimensions imposed by coherence time and end-to-end latency budgets, making the blocklength penalty a primary design consideration rather than an asymptotic correction as in traditional wireless communication system design. However, the BLER expression derived in~\cite{Polyanskiy2010} is non-linear, motivating tractable piecewise-linear BLER approximations for fading channels, as developed in~\cite{Makki2014}. In addition, finite-blocklength outage bounds for OTFS were derived in~\cite{Zhang2024} by reformulating the problem as an equivalent parallel AWGN channel. However, the analysis of~\cite{Zhang2024} is restricted to generic terrestrial channels and does not capture the fading channel characteristics or the relay structure of LEO NTN OTFS links, nor does it connect the resulting BLER expressions to a diversity-transform design.

\subsection{Motivations and Contributions}
\label{sec:motivations_contributions}

The following research gaps motivate this work. First, existing
multi-satellite diversity techniques rely on statistical combining
and cannot certify data recovery under arbitrary block-erasure
patterns. Although the diversity-transform framework provides
provable full-rank recovery guarantees under block-aligned
erasures, no prior work applies this framework to a practical
satellite communication system. Second, no finite-blocklength
BLER analysis exists for OTFS-based satellite links. Such an
analysis is essential for quantifying the reliability of
mission-critical NTN channels operating under stringent latency
constraints, where the blocklength penalty is a primary design
consideration rather than an asymptotic correction. Third, while
the algebraic foundation for diversity transforms is established
in the literature, no prior work analyses the behaviour of
diversity transforms over subchannels with heterogeneous
reliability levels, nor develops a mechanism to exploit predicted
subchannel reliability for performance optimisation while
preserving outage recovery guarantees. This paper addresses all
three gaps by developing a comprehensive diversity-transform
architecture for extreme-reliability LEO NTN OTFS downlinks.
The main contributions are summarised as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Diversity Transform for NTN Communications:}
A diversity transform is introduced for OTFS-modulated LEO NTN downlinks, 
together with a systematic construction of binary diversity transform matrices 
derived from Reed--Solomon codes. The design guarantees full-rank recovery 
of all encoded subwords whenever a sufficient number of subchannels survive, 
as established in Theorem~\ref{thm:block_mds}.

\item \textit{Finite-Blocklength BLER Analysis for OTFS-based NTN Links:}
The end-to-end finite-blocklength block error rate (BLER) is derived for 
OTFS modulation over a transparent satellite relay in non-terrestrial networks 
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fbl_bler}). Tractable closed-form approximations 
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bler_approx}) and a high-SNR diversity-order expression 
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bler_high_snr}) are also provided, explicitly revealing 
the dependence of BLER on the Rician K-factor, the number of resolvable 
delay--Doppler paths, and the blocklength.

\item \textit{Adaptive Diversity Transform:}
An adaptive diversity transform scheme is proposed that uses predicted 
per-subchannel reliability scores to allocate critical transform outputs 
to the most reliable subchannels. A family of adaptive transforms is 
constructed by left-multiplying the baseline matrix with invertible binary 
matrices from the general linear group. All adaptive transforms preserve 
the baseline's full-rank outage recovery guarantees 
(Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive_rank_preserve}). Under ephemeris-derived 
predicted subchannel reliability scores, optimal selection of the diversity 
transform strictly reduces the average block error rate compared to the 
fixed baseline transform (Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive_perf_gain}).

\item \textit{Ephemeris-Driven Outage Prediction and Transform Selection:}
An ephemeris-driven reliability prediction framework is developed that 
combines orbital mechanics, atmospheric models, and the derived 
finite-blocklength BLER to compute per-subchannel reliability scores. 
Algorithm~\ref{alg:adaptive_transform_selection} selects the optimal 
rank-preserving transform by maximising a reliability-weighted quality 
metric, concentrating information on predicted reliable subchannels while 
maintaining full recovery guarantees for all predicted erasure patterns.

\item \textit{Polar Code Integration with Diversity Transform:}
The combined polar--diversity generator matrix is shown to admit a 
Kronecker-product structure, yielding a single rectangular code that jointly 
provides forward error correction and diversity protection. An iterative 
decoding architecture between successive-cancellation-list polar decoding 
and diversity demapping is formalised for LEO NTN OTFS channels.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system_model}
We consider a LEO satellite communication system in which a
constellation of satellites provides continuous downlink coverage to a moving
user equipment (UE) on the ground, with connectivity enabled via a gateway
station (GS), as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:intro_leo_ntn}. Let
$k \in \{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{S}\}$ denote a transmitting network node,
corresponding to the gateway station (GS) or a satellite (S), and let
$\ell \in \{\mathrm{S},\mathrm{U}\}$ denote the corresponding receiving node.
At any given time, the UE is simultaneously served by a subset of
$N_{\text{serv}} \ge 1$ LEO satellites using a multi-connectivity protocol to
realise satellite diversity. The UE position is denoted by $\vect{u}(t)$, while
the $N_{\text{serv}}$ serving satellites occupy distinct locations
$\vect{s}_i(t)$, $i \in \{1,\ldots,N_{\text{serv}}\}$. Each satellite--UE pair 
defines an instantaneous slant range
$d_i(t) = \|\vect{s}_i(t) - \vect{u}(t)\|$ and a line-of-sight (LOS) direction
vector
$\vect{k}_i(t) = (\vect{s}_i(t) - \vect{u}(t))/d_i(t)$. The elevation angle of
satellite~$i$ as observed at the UE is given by
$\epsilon_i(t)=\arcsin\!\bigl(\vect{k}_i(t) \cdot \vect{e}_{\mathrm{vert}}\bigr)$,
where $\vect{e}_{\mathrm{vert}}$ denotes the local zenith unit vector at the UE.
Downlink connectivity is realised through $n = N_{\text{serv}}$ parallel 
subchannels, where each subchannel $i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$ corresponds to a 
complete end-to-end path through a distinct satellite~$i$, comprising both the 
feeder link and access link. Each subchannel operates as a frequency-separated 
OTFS carrier, and the spatial separation of satellites ensures statistically 
independent propagation across subchannels.
To enhance reliability over these diverse links, a diversity-transform layer is
implemented at the gateway station. The source bitstream is first encoded by a
forward error-correction (FEC) encoder into $\varrho$ information subwords, which are
then linearly transformed into $n_{\text{out}} > \varrho$ redundant outputs using a binary
diversity matrix
$\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{DIV}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$. The resulting $n_{\text{out}}$ transform outputs are mapped onto the $n$ physical 
subchannels according to a fixed assignment $\mathcal{A} = (A_1,\ldots,A_n)$, 
where each subchannel carries $|A_i| = m$ transform outputs corresponding 
to one field-symbol block. These 
transform outputs are OTFS modulated at the gateway and forwarded to the 
corresponding satellites via the feeder links. Each satellite employs a 
transparent (bent-pipe) payload that amplifies and frequency-converts the 
received signal without demodulation or decoding, then retransmits on the access 
link toward the UE. This transparent relay architecture ensures that the 
algebraic structure of $\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{DIV}}$ is inherently preserved, as 
the transform outputs pass through the satellites unaltered. On each subchannel, 
the transmitted symbols are arranged on an $N_D \times M_D$ delay--Doppler grid 
and modulated using OTFS.

After OTFS demodulation and equalisation at the UE, a subchannel is declared to
be in outage when its end-to-end error probability exceeds a target threshold 
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}$. Since each satellite operates as a transparent relay, 
the feeder-link noise propagates through to the access link, and the end-to-end 
subchannel is characterised by a cascaded channel model with a single decoding 
operation performed at the UE. An ephemeris-driven open-loop predictor is 
employed to estimate future satellite and UE states at time $t + T_{\Delta}$, 
where $T_{\Delta}$ denotes the total system latency. Each subchannel $i$ 
comprises a gateway--satellite feeder link and a satellite--UE access link 
operating in cascade. Using the predicted ephemerides and large-scale channel 
states $\tilde{G}_{i,GS}^{\mathrm{LS}}$ and $\tilde{G}_{i,SU}^{\mathrm{LS}}$, 
the end-to-end effective SNR $\tilde{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}$ is computed 
from the cascaded link model, and the associated finite-blocklength error 
probability yields the predicted reliability of subchannel~$i$. Subchannels 
satisfying $\epsilon_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}} > \epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}$ are modelled 
as block erasures in the diversity-transform domain. As detailed in 
Section~\ref{sec:diversity_transform}, an adaptive diversity transform 
$\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{DIV}}$ is selected to guarantee full-rank recovery for all 
predicted erasure patterns, ensuring recoverability of the $\varrho$ information 
subwords. Finally, the UE performs diversity demapping followed by FEC decoding.

\subsection{Channel Model}
\label{sec:channel-model}

Each subchannel $i \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$ represents a complete end-to-end path through satellite~$i$, comprising a gateway--satellite feeder link ($G$--$S_i$) and a satellite--UE access link ($S_i$--$U$) operating in cascade. Under the transparent (bent-pipe) relay architecture, the satellite amplifies and forwards the received signal without demodulation, so the feeder-link noise propagates through to the access link and both channels contribute to the end-to-end impairment. For each link $k$--$\ell$ within subchannel~$i$, where $k \in \{G, S\}$ and $\ell \in \{S, U\}$, the propagation follows a doubly selective tapped-delay-line Doppler (TDL--D) fading model that incorporates both large-scale and small-scale effects.

The complex baseband impulse response of the $k$--$\ell$ link within subchannel~$i$ at time $t$ and delay $\tau$ is given by
\begin{equation}
h_{i,k\ell}(t,\tau) = \sum_{p=0}^{L_{i,k\ell}-1} \alpha_{i,k\ell,p}(t)\, \delta(\tau-\tau_{i,k\ell,p}),
\label{eq:ntn_impulse_response}
\end{equation}
where $L_{i,k\ell}$ denotes the number of resolvable taps, $\tau_{i,k\ell,p}$ is the delay of the $p$th path, and $\alpha_{i,k\ell,p}(t)$ is the corresponding complex gain. Consistent with the TDL--D framework, each tap gain is decomposed into a large-scale component and a small-scale Rician fading term according to
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{i,k\ell,p}(t) = \sqrt{G_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}(t) \cdot P_{i,k\ell,p}}\, g_{i,k\ell,p}(t),
\label{eq:tap_gain_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $P_{i,k\ell,p}$ is the TDL--D relative tap power satisfying the normalisation condition $\sum_{p=0}^{L_{i,k\ell}-1} P_{i,k\ell,p} = 1$, and $G_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}(t)$ is the linear large-scale gain. The small-scale fading coefficient is modelled as
\begin{equation}
g_{i,k\ell,p}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{K_{i,k\ell}}{K_{i,k\ell}+1}}\, e^{j2\pi f_{D,i,k\ell,p}t} + \sqrt{\frac{1}{K_{i,k\ell}+1}}\, w_{i,k\ell,p}(t),
\label{eq:rician_fading}
\end{equation}
with $K_{i,k\ell}$ denoting the Rician factor, $f_{D,i,k\ell,p}$ the Doppler shift of tap $p$, and $w_{i,k\ell,p}(t)\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)$ representing the diffuse multipath component. The Doppler shift for each tap is determined by
\begin{equation}
f_{D,i,k\ell,p} = \frac{v_{\mathrm{rel},i,k\ell}(t)}{\lambda} \cos\theta_{i,k\ell,p}(t),
\label{eq:doppler_shift}
\end{equation}
where $v_{\mathrm{rel},i,k\ell}(t)$ denotes the radial relative velocity between nodes $k$ and $\ell$, $\lambda = c/f_c$ is the carrier wavelength, and $\theta_{i,k\ell,p}(t)$ is the angle of arrival of the $p$th path.

The large-scale path loss aggregates multiple propagation effects and can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PL}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{tot}}(t) = \mathrm{PL}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{FS}}(t) + \mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{atm}}(t) + \mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}(t),
\label{eq:total_path_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{PL}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{FS}}(t)$ is the free-space path loss, $\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}(t)$ is the combined blockage and shadowing loss, and the aggregate atmospheric attenuation is given by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{atm}}(t) \triangleq \mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{gas}}(t) + \mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{cf}}(t) + \mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{rain}}(t),
\label{eq:atm_loss}
\end{equation}
comprising gaseous absorption, cloud and fog attenuation, and rain fading contributions. The free-space path loss follows the standard propagation model
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{PL}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{FS}}(t) = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi d_{i,k\ell}(t)}{\lambda}\right),
\label{eq:fspl}
\end{equation}
where $d_{i,k\ell}(t)$ is the slant range of the $k$--$\ell$ link. For the access link, the slant range can be expressed in terms of the elevation angle as
\begin{equation}
d_{i,SU}(t) = R_E\biggl( \sqrt{\Bigl(1+\frac{h_{\mathrm{orb}}}{R_E}\Bigr)^2 -\cos^2\epsilon_i(t)} -\sin\epsilon_i(t) \biggr),
\label{eq:slant_range}
\end{equation}
where $R_E$ is the Earth radius, $h_{\mathrm{orb}}$ is the orbital altitude, and $\epsilon_i(t)$ is the elevation angle of satellite~$i$ as observed at the UE.

Gaseous absorption is modelled according to ITU-R P.676 with specific attenuation
\begin{equation}
\gamma_i^{\mathrm{gas}}(t) = \gamma_{\mathrm{O_2}}(f_c,P_{\mathrm{atm}},T_{\mathrm{atm}}) + \gamma_{\mathrm{H_2O}}(f_c,P_{\mathrm{atm}},T_{\mathrm{atm}},\rho_i(t)),
\label{eq:gas_specific}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\mathrm{O_2}}$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{H_2O}}$ are the oxygen and water-vapour absorption coefficients, respectively, $P_{\mathrm{atm}}$ is the atmospheric pressure, $T_{\mathrm{atm}}$ is the temperature, and $\rho_i(t)$ is the water-vapour density. The total gaseous attenuation along the slant path is then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{gas}}(t) = \gamma_i^{\mathrm{gas}}(t)\, \frac{H_{\mathrm{gas}}}{\sin\epsilon_{i,k\ell}(t)},
\label{eq:gas_loss}
\end{equation}
where $H_{\mathrm{gas}}$ is the equivalent atmospheric thickness. Cloud and fog attenuation is computed following ITU-R P.840~\cite{ITUcloud}, with specific attenuation given by
\begin{equation}
\gamma_i^{\mathrm{cf}}(t) = K_l(f_c)\,\mathrm{LWC}_i(t),
\label{eq:cf_specific}
\end{equation}
where $K_l(f_c)$ is the specific liquid-water absorption coefficient and $\mathrm{LWC}_i(t)$ is the liquid-water content in g/m$^3$. The resulting cloud and fog attenuation is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{cf}}(t) = \gamma_i^{\mathrm{cf}}(t)\, \frac{H_{\mathrm{cf}}}{\sin\epsilon_{i,k\ell}(t)},
\label{eq:cf_loss}
\end{equation}
where $H_{\mathrm{cf}}$ denotes the cloud-layer thickness. Rain attenuation is modelled according to ITU-R P.618 as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{rain}}(t) = \kappa\,R_i(t)^{\beta}\, d_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{rain}}(t),
\label{eq:rain_loss}
\end{equation}
where $R_i(t)$ is the rain rate in mm/h, $\kappa$ and $\beta$ are frequency-dependent attenuation coefficients, and the effective rain path length is given by
\begin{equation}
d_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{rain}}(t) = \frac{H_{\mathrm{rain}} - h_{\mathrm{UE}}}{\sin\epsilon_{i,k\ell}(t)} \cdot r_{i}(t),
\label{eq:rain_path}
\end{equation}
with $H_{\mathrm{rain}}$ being the rain height, $h_{\mathrm{UE}}$ the UE altitude, and $r_{i}(t)$ the path reduction factor.

The blockage and shadowing loss $\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}(t)$ is governed by a binary line-of-sight (LOS) indicator $B_{i,k\ell}(t)\in\{0,1\}$, where $B_{i,k\ell}(t)=1$ indicates an unobstructed link and $B_{i,k\ell}(t)=0$ denotes deep blockage. The indicator follows a Bernoulli distribution with $\mathbb{P}[B_{i,k\ell}(t)=1] = P_{\mathrm{LOS}}(\epsilon_{i,k\ell}(t),\xi)$, where $\xi \in \{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{S}, \mathrm{R}\}$ denotes urban, suburban, or rural environment, respectively. The LOS probability follows the 3GPP NTN model~\cite{3GPP38811}:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{LOS}}(\epsilon,\xi) = \begin{cases} 1, & \epsilon \ge \epsilon_{\mathrm{max}}(\xi), \\[4pt] \exp\!\left(-\dfrac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{max}}(\xi)-\epsilon}{\theta(\xi)}\right), & \epsilon_{\mathrm{min}} \le \epsilon < \epsilon_{\mathrm{max}}(\xi), \\[8pt] P_{\mathrm{base}}(\xi), & \epsilon < \epsilon_{\mathrm{min}}, \end{cases}
\label{eq:los_prob}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{min}}$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{max}}(\xi)$, $\theta(\xi)$, and $P_{\mathrm{base}}(\xi)$ are environment-specific parameters. Conditioned on the blockage state, the blockage loss is expressed as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}(t) = \begin{cases} \mu_{\mathrm{sh}} + X_{i,k\ell}(t), & B_{i,k\ell}(t)=1, \\[4pt] \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{deep}}, & B_{i,k\ell}(t)=0, \end{cases}
\label{eq:blockage}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\mathrm{sh}}$ is the mean shadowing margin, $X_{i,k\ell}(t)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\mathrm{sh}}^2)$ models lognormal shadow fading, and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{deep}}$ represents the deep-blockage loss. The linear large-scale channel gain is obtained from the total path loss according to
\begin{equation}
G_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}(t) = 10^{-\mathrm{PL}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{tot}}(t)/10}.
\label{eq:large_scale_gain}
\end{equation}

Having established the large-scale channel characterisation, the instantaneous signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for the $k$--$\ell$ link of subchannel $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{i,k\ell}(t)
= \frac{P_k \mathcal{G}_k G_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}(t) \mathcal{G}_\ell}
{\sigma_{n,\ell}^2}
|g_{i,k\ell}(t)|^2
\label{eq:snr_generic}
\end{equation}
where $P_k$ is the transmit power at node $k$, $\mathcal{G}_k$ and $\mathcal{G}_\ell$ are the antenna gains at the transmitting and receiving nodes, $\sigma_{n,\ell}^2$ is the noise power at receiving node $\ell$, and $|g_{i,k\ell}(t)|^2$ is the small-scale fading power.

The average SNR follows by taking the expectation of \eqref{eq:snr_generic} over the small-scale fading. Under the Rician model in \eqref{eq:rician_fading}, the normalisation condition
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\bigl[|g_{i,k\ell}(t)|^2\bigr] 
= \frac{K_{i,k\ell}}{K_{i,k\ell}+1} + \frac{1}{K_{i,k\ell}+1} = 1
\label{eq:fading_normalisation}
\end{equation}
holds, and the average SNR becomes
\begin{equation}
\bar{\gamma}_{i,k\ell}(t)
= \frac{P_k \mathcal{G}_k G_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}(t) \mathcal{G}_\ell}
{\sigma_{n,\ell}^2}.
\label{eq:avg_snr_generic}
\end{equation}

Under the transparent relay architecture, the satellite employs automatic gain control (AGC) to maintain constant output power. Consequently, the end-to-end effective SNR for subchannel~$i$ is determined by the cascaded combination of both links. Following the analysis in~\cite{Hasna2004}, this cascaded SNR is given by
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}(t) = \frac{\gamma_{i,GS}(t) \cdot \gamma_{i,SU}(t)}{\gamma_{i,GS}(t) + \gamma_{i,SU}(t) + 1}.
\label{eq:cascaded_snr}
\end{equation}
This expression captures the noise amplification effect inherent to transparent relaying, where the feeder-link noise is amplified and forwarded along with the desired signal. The average end-to-end SNR can be approximated as
\begin{equation}
\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}(t) \approx \frac{\bar{\gamma}_{i,GS}(t) \cdot \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t)}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,GS}(t) + \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t) + 1}.
\label{eq:avg_cascaded_snr}
\end{equation}
In the high-SNR regime, \eqref{eq:cascaded_snr} simplifies to $\gamma_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx \min(\gamma_{i,GS}, \gamma_{i,SU})$, indicating that the weaker of the two links dominates the end-to-end performance. The feeder link employs high-gain tracking antennas at both the gateway and satellite, typically operating in Ka-band or Q-band with antenna gains exceeding 40~dBi. Due to the narrow beamwidth and absence of significant scatterers at orbital altitudes, the feeder-link channel exhibits negligible delay spread ($L_{i,GS} = 1$) and a dominant LOS component ($K_{i,GS} \gg 1$). Consequently, the feeder-link SNR is quasi-deterministic:
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{i,GS}(t) \approx \bar{\gamma}_{i,GS}(t).
\label{eq:feeder_deterministic}
\end{equation}

In contrast, the access link experiences multipath propagation due to scattering in the UE's local environment, yielding $L_i = L_{i,SU} \geq 1$ resolvable paths. The delay--Doppler diversity exploited by OTFS modulation therefore arises entirely from the access-link multipath structure. The per-path access-link SNR is
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{i,SU,p}(t) = \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t) \cdot P_{i,SU,p} \cdot |g_{i,SU,p}(t)|^2,
\label{eq:per_path_access_snr}
\end{equation}
where $P_{i,SU,p}$ is the relative power of the $p$th tap and $|g_{i,SU,p}(t)|^2$ is the corresponding small-scale fading power.


OTFS modulation enables the receiver to resolve and coherently combine the $L_i$ access-link paths through delay--Doppler domain processing. Under the transparent relay architecture, the multipath components resolved at the receiver share a common amplified feeder-link noise contribution, since the satellite amplifies and forwards the entire received signal including noise. Consequently, after maximum-ratio combining (MRC) across the $L_i$ resolved access-link paths, the effective end-to-end SNR is given by
\begin{equation}
\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}(t) = \frac{\bar{\gamma}_{i,GS}(t) \cdot \gamma_{i,SU}^{\mathrm{MRC}}(t)}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,GS}(t) + \gamma_{i,SU}^{\mathrm{MRC}}(t) + 1},
\label{eq:combined_snr_full}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{i,SU}^{\mathrm{MRC}}(t) = \sum_{p=0}^{L_i-1} \gamma_{i,SU,p}(t)
\label{eq:mrc_access_snr}
\end{equation}
denotes the MRC-combined access-link SNR. This formulation accounts for the correlated feeder-link noise across all resolved taps while the independent access-link noise contributions add incoherently under MRC. In the typical operating regime where the feeder link employs high-gain tracking antennas and operates with substantially higher SNR than the access link, i.e., $\bar{\gamma}_{i,GS} \gg \gamma_{i,SU}^{\mathrm{MRC}}$, the cascaded expression simplifies to
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}(t) &\approx& \gamma_{i,SU}^{\mathrm{MRC}}(t) = \sum_{p=0}^{L_i-1} \gamma_{i,SU,p}(t) \nonumber\\
&=& \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t) \sum_{p=0}^{L_i-1} P_{i,SU,p} \cdot |g_{i,SU,p}(t)|^2.
\label{eq:combined_snr_approx}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}

For LEO satellite channels operating in rural or suburban environments, the Rician K-factor of the LOS path is typically large~\cite{3GPP_TR38811}, indicating a dominant LOS component. Under this dominant-LOS approximation ($K_{i,SU,0} \gg 1$), the first tap contributes a quasi-deterministic SNR
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}} = \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t) \cdot P_{i,SU,0},
\label{eq:los_deterministic}
\end{equation}
while the remaining $L_i - 1$ NLOS paths exhibit Rayleigh fading ($K_{i,SU,p} = 0$ for $p \geq 1$). The aggregate NLOS component
\begin{equation}
\gamma_i^{\mathrm{NLOS}}(t) = \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t) \sum_{p=1}^{L_i-1} P_{i,SU,p} \cdot |g_{i,SU,p}(t)|^2
\label{eq:nlos_aggregate}
\end{equation}
follows a gamma distribution with shape parameter $L_i - 1$ and scale parameter $\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}$, where $\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}} = \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t) \cdot P_{\mathrm{NLOS}} / (L_i - 1)$ and $P_{\mathrm{NLOS}} = \sum_{p=1}^{L_i-1} P_{i,SU,p} = 1 - P_{i,SU,0}$. The combined SNR after MRC is therefore
\begin{equation}
\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}(t) = \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}} + \gamma_i^{\mathrm{NLOS}}(t),
\label{eq:combined_snr_final}
\end{equation}
which follows a shifted gamma distribution with a deterministic offset $\gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$ and a random component $\gamma_i^{\mathrm{NLOS}}(t)$. The total average SNR satisfies the power budget
\begin{equation}
\bar{\gamma}_i^{\mathrm{c}} \approx \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}} + (L_i - 1) \bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}} \approx \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}(t),
\label{eq:snr_power_budget}
\end{equation}
under the strong-feeder-link approximation, where noise amplification in the transparent relay becomes negligible. This unified channel model, incorporating the transparent relay structure with dominant feeder link and the OTFS-enabled multipath diversity from the access link, provides the foundation for the finite-blocklength reliability analysis presented in the following sections.

\subsection{OTFS Modulation}
\label{sec:otfs}

Building upon the channel model established in Section~\ref{sec:channel-model}, this subsection presents the orthogonal time-frequency space (OTFS) modulation framework that exploits the delay--Doppler channel structure to achieve diversity across the $L_i$ resolvable paths. Under the transparent relay architecture, OTFS modulation is performed at the gateway station, and the modulated signal propagates through the cascaded feeder--satellite--access path, characterised by the end-to-end SNR in \eqref{eq:cascaded_snr}, to the UE where OTFS demodulation is performed.

On each physical subchannel~$i$, OTFS modulation maps information symbols onto a two-dimensional delay--Doppler grid of size $N_{\mathrm{D}} \times M_{\mathrm{D}}$, where $N_{\mathrm{D}}$ and $M_{\mathrm{D}}$ represent the numbers of Doppler and delay bins, respectively. Let $x[u,v]$, with $u = 0,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{D}}-1$ and $v = 0,\ldots,M_{\mathrm{D}}-1$, denote the quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) symbols placed on this grid. The inverse symplectic finite Fourier transform (ISFFT) maps these delay--Doppler domain symbols into the time--frequency domain according to
\begin{equation}
X[p,q] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{D}}M_{\mathrm{D}}}} \sum_{u=0}^{N_{\mathrm{D}}-1} \sum_{v=0}^{M_{\mathrm{D}}-1} x[u,v]\, e^{j2\pi\left(\frac{pu}{N_{\mathrm{D}}} - \frac{qv}{M_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)},
\label{eq:isfft}
\end{equation}
for $p = 0,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{D}}-1$ and $q = 0,\ldots,M_{\mathrm{D}}-1$, where $p$ and $q$ denote the discrete time and frequency indices, respectively.

The continuous-time transmit signal at the gateway is subsequently obtained via the Heisenberg transform as
\begin{equation}
s(t) = \sum_{p=0}^{N_{\mathrm{D}}-1} \sum_{q=0}^{M_{\mathrm{D}}-1} X[p,q]\,g_{\mathrm{tx}}(t-pT_{\mathrm{sym}})\, e^{j2\pi q \Delta f (t-pT_{\mathrm{sym}})},
\label{eq:tx_signal}
\end{equation}
where $g_{\mathrm{tx}}(\cdot)$ is the transmit pulse-shaping function, $T_{\mathrm{sym}}$ is the OTFS symbol duration, and $\Delta f$ is the subcarrier spacing satisfying the orthogonality condition $\Delta f \cdot T_{\mathrm{sym}} = 1$.

At the UE receiver, OTFS demodulation is performed by applying the Wigner transform followed by the symplectic finite Fourier transform (SFFT). The delay--Doppler domain received symbols are obtained as
\begin{equation}
y_{i}[u,v] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{D}}M_{\mathrm{D}}}} \sum_{p=0}^{N_{\mathrm{D}}-1} \sum_{q=0}^{M_{\mathrm{D}}-1} Y_{i}[p,q]\, e^{-j2\pi\left(\frac{pu}{N_{\mathrm{D}}} - \frac{qv}{M_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)},
\label{eq:sfft}
\end{equation}
where $Y_{i}[p,q]$ denotes the time--frequency domain received samples after matched filtering.

Under standard pulse-shaping and sampling assumptions, the delay--Doppler input--output relation for subchannel~$i$ can be expressed in discrete form as
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
y_{i}[u,v] &=& \sum_{u'=0}^{N_{\mathrm{D}}-1} \sum_{v'=0}^{M_{\mathrm{D}}-1} h_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}\!\left[(u-u')_{N_{\mathrm{D}}}, (v-v')_{M_{\mathrm{D}}}\right] \nonumber\\
&& \times\, x[u',v'] + w_{i}[u,v]
\label{eq:dd_io}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where $(\cdot)_{N_{\mathrm{D}}}$ and $(\cdot)_{M_{\mathrm{D}}}$ denote modulo-$N_{\mathrm{D}}$ and modulo-$M_{\mathrm{D}}$ operations, respectively, and $w_{i}[u,v] \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, \sigma_{w,i}^2)$ is the effective noise. Under the transparent relay architecture, the effective noise comprises the access-link thermal noise and the amplified feeder-link noise. In the dominant feeder-link regime where $\bar{\gamma}_{i,GS} \gg \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}$, the amplified feeder-link noise contribution becomes negligible, and the effective noise variance is well-approximated by
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{w,i}^2 \approx \sigma_{n,U}^2,
\label{eq:effective_noise_approx}
\end{equation}
indicating that the access-link noise dominates the end-to-end noise floor. This result is consistent with the cascaded SNR approximation in~\eqref{eq:combined_snr_approx}, where the end-to-end performance is limited by the access link.

Similarly, since the feeder link exhibits negligible delay--Doppler dispersion ($L_{i,GS} = 1$), the effective end-to-end kernel simplifies to $h_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[u,v] = \alpha_{i,GS} \cdot h_{i,SU}[u,v]$, where $h_{i,SU}[u,v]$ denotes the access-link delay--Doppler kernel with $L_i$ resolvable taps. The blocklength $n_c = N_{\mathrm{D}} M_{\mathrm{D}}$, together with the cascaded SNR model in~\eqref{eq:combined_snr_full}, provides the foundation for the reliability analysis developed in Section~\ref{sec:reliability_analysis}.




\section{Reliability Analysis and Outage Prediction}
\label{sec:reliability_analysis}

This section develops the reliability analysis framework for the LEO NTN OTFS system. Building upon the channel model and OTFS modulation framework established in Section~\ref{sec:system_model}, we first derive the finite-blocklength block error probability (BLER) that accounts for delay--Doppler diversity, then present an ephemeris-driven prediction mechanism that enables proactive diversity transform selection.

\subsection{Finite-Blocklength Block Error Probability}
\label{sec:finite-blocklength}

This subsection derives the finite-blocklength BLER for the OTFS downlink over the transparent satellite relay characterised in Section~\ref{sec:channel-model}. The analysis builds upon the unified channel model developed therein, which captures both the cascaded relay structure and the OTFS-enabled multipath diversity, and incorporates the blocklength penalty that arises when operating at rates close to channel capacity.

OTFS modulation exploits the channel's delay--Doppler structure to achieve diversity across the $L_i$ resolvable access-link paths. For an OTFS frame of size $N_{\mathrm{D}} \times M_{\mathrm{D}}$ with blocklength $n_c = N_{\mathrm{D}} M_{\mathrm{D}}$, the achievable diversity order is $d_i = \min(L_i, N_{\mathrm{D}}, M_{\mathrm{D}})$. For typical OTFS parameters where the frame dimensions significantly exceed the number of resolvable paths, i.e., $N_{\mathrm{D}}, M_{\mathrm{D}} \gg L_i$, the diversity order simplifies to $d_i = L_i$, indicating that OTFS fully exploits the available multipath diversity under ideal conditions. In practice, fractional Doppler and imperfect channel estimation often reduce the effective diversity order.

As established in Section~\ref{sec:channel-model}, under the dominant feeder-link assumption and MRC processing in the delay--Doppler domain, the combined SNR $\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}$ in~\eqref{eq:combined_snr_final} follows a shifted gamma distribution. The LOS path contributes a quasi-deterministic component $\gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$ given by~\eqref{eq:los_deterministic}, while the aggregate NLOS component $\gamma_i^{\mathrm{NLOS}}$ in~\eqref{eq:nlos_aggregate} follows a gamma distribution with shape parameter $L_i - 1$ and scale parameter $\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}$:
\begin{equation}
f_{\gamma_{i}^{\mathrm{NLOS}}}(\gamma) = \frac{\gamma^{L_i-2} e^{-\gamma/\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}}{\Gamma(L_i-1)(\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}})^{L_i-1}}, \quad \gamma \geq 0.
\label{eq:nlos_pdf}
\end{equation}

To express the CDF of the combined SNR concisely, we define the regularised lower incomplete gamma function as
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon(a,x) \triangleq \frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_0^x t^{a-1} e^{-t} \, dt.
\label{eq:reg_inc_gamma_def}
\end{equation}
The CDF of the combined SNR is then
\begin{equation}
F_{\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}}(\gamma) = \Upsilon\left(L_i-1, \frac{\gamma - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}\right), \quad \gamma \geq \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}},
\label{eq:combined_cdf}
\end{equation}
and $F_{\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}}(\gamma) = 0$ for $\gamma < \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$.

The outage probability at SNR threshold $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} = 2^{R_c} - 1$, where $R_c$ denotes the coding rate in bits per channel use, is given by
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(\infty)} = F_{\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}}(\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}) = \Upsilon\left(L_i-1, \frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}\right),
\label{eq:outage_combined}
\end{equation}
for $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} > \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$, and $P_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(\infty)} = 0$ otherwise. This characterises performance in the asymptotic regime of infinite blocklength. For finite blocklength $n_c$, the BLER is elevated above the outage probability due to the backoff from capacity required for reliable decoding.

Following the finite-blocklength framework established in~\cite{Polyanskiy2010} and its extension to quasi-static fading channels in~\cite{Yang2014}, the maximum achievable rate at blocklength $n_c$ and error probability $\epsilon$ over a channel with instantaneous SNR $\gamma$ is characterised by the normal approximation:
\begin{equation}
R^*(n_c, \epsilon, \gamma) \approx C(\gamma) - \sqrt{\frac{V(\gamma)}{n_c}}\, Q^{-1}(\epsilon) + \frac{\log_2 n_c}{2 n_c},
\label{eq:normal_approx}
\end{equation}
where the instantaneous capacity and channel dispersion are
\begin{equation}
C(\gamma) = \log_2(1 + \gamma), \qquad V(\gamma) = \left(1 - \frac{1}{(1+\gamma)^2}\right)(\log_2 e)^2,
\label{eq:capacity_dispersion}
\end{equation}
and $Q^{-1}(\cdot)$ denotes the inverse of the Gaussian Q-function. Inverting the normal approximation and averaging over the fading distribution yields the following characterisation.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Blocklength BLER for OTFS over Diversity Channel]
\label{thm:fbl_bler}
Consider an OTFS transmission with blocklength $n_c = N_{\mathrm{D}} M_{\mathrm{D}}$, coding rate $R_c$ bits per channel use, and $L_i \geq 1$ resolvable delay--Doppler paths on subchannel~$i$. Under the transparent satellite relay architecture with dominant feeder link and MRC diversity combining as characterised in Section~\ref{sec:channel-model}, the average end-to-end BLER is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} = \mathbb{E}_{\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}}\left[Q\left(\frac{\sqrt{n_c}\left(C(\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}) - R_c\right) + \frac{\log_2 n_c}{2\sqrt{n_c}}}{\sqrt{V(\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}})}}\right)\right],
\label{eq:bler_exact}
\end{equation}
where the expectation is taken over the combined SNR $\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}$ with CDF given by~\eqref{eq:combined_cdf}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
For a quasi-static fading channel with instantaneous SNR $\gamma$, the conditional error probability under optimal coding at rate $R_c$ satisfies $\epsilon(\gamma) \approx Q\left(\frac{n_c C(\gamma) - n_c R_c + \frac{1}{2}\log_2 n_c}{\sqrt{n_c V(\gamma)}}\right)$ for sufficiently large $n_c$~\cite{Yang2014}. Under MRC diversity combining with the dominant feeder-link and dominant-LOS approximations established in Section~\ref{sec:channel-model}, the effective SNR is $\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}$ as given by~\eqref{eq:combined_snr_final}. Averaging over the shifted gamma distribution yields~\eqref{eq:bler_exact}.
\end{IEEEproof}

In the limit $n_c \to \infty$, the Q-function converges to a step function and the BLER approaches the outage probability: $\lim_{n_c \to \infty} \epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} = P_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(\infty)}$. For any finite $n_c$, the BLER strictly exceeds the outage probability.

Due to the complexity of the Q-function in~\eqref{eq:bler_exact}, obtaining a closed-form expression for the BLER is challenging. To address this, we employ the piecewise linear approximation technique developed in~\cite{Makki2014,Basnayaka2022}. The conditional error probability can be approximated as
\begin{equation}
\Theta_i(\gamma_i) =
\begin{cases}
1, & \gamma_i \leq \phi_i, \\
\frac{1}{2} - \beta_i \sqrt{n_c}(\gamma_i - \psi_i), & \phi_i < \gamma_i < \delta_i, \\
0, & \gamma_i \geq \delta_i,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:piecewise_approx}
\end{equation}
where the approximation parameters are defined as
\begin{align}
\psi_i &= 2^{R_c} - 1 = \gamma_{\mathrm{th}}, \label{eq:psi_def}\\
\beta_i &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(2^{2R_c} - 1)}}, \label{eq:beta_def}\\
\phi_i &= \psi_i - \frac{1}{2\beta_i \sqrt{n_c}}, \label{eq:phi_def}\\
\delta_i &= \psi_i + \frac{1}{2\beta_i \sqrt{n_c}}. \label{eq:delta_def}
\end{align}
Here, $\psi_i$ represents the SNR threshold corresponding to the target rate $R_c$, and the interval $[\phi_i, \delta_i]$ defines the transition region of width $1/(\beta_i\sqrt{n_c})$ centred at the threshold.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-Form BLER Approximation]
\label{prop:bler_approx}
Using the piecewise linear approximation~\eqref{eq:piecewise_approx}, the finite-blocklength BLER for subchannel~$i$ with MRC diversity combining under the dominant-LOS approximation is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx F_{\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}}(\psi_i) = \Upsilon\left(L_i-1, \frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}\right),
\label{eq:bler_cdf_combined}
\end{equation}
for $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} > \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Substituting the piecewise approximation~\eqref{eq:piecewise_approx} into the expectation~\eqref{eq:bler_exact} and following the approach in~\cite{Makki2014}, the BLER can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx \beta_i\sqrt{n_c} \int_{\phi_i}^{\delta_i} F_{\gamma_i^{\mathrm{c}}}(\gamma_i) \, d\gamma_i.
\end{equation}
For sufficiently large $n_c$, the transition region $[\phi_i, \delta_i]$ becomes narrow, and the integral can be approximated by evaluating the CDF at the midpoint $\psi_i = \gamma_{\mathrm{th}}$, yielding~\eqref{eq:bler_cdf_combined}.
\end{IEEEproof}

Using the series expansion of the regularised incomplete gamma function, the closed-form BLER approximation can be expressed as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx 1 - e^{-\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}} \sum_{m=0}^{L_i-2} \frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}\right)^m,
\label{eq:bler_explicit}
\end{equation}
for $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} > \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$, and $\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx 0$ otherwise.

To gain further insight into the diversity behaviour, we derive a simplified high-SNR approximation that explicitly reveals the diversity order.

\begin{proposition}[High-SNR BLER Approximation]
\label{prop:bler_high_snr}
In the high-SNR regime where $\gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}} \gg \gamma_{\mathrm{th}}$ or equivalently when the normalised threshold $\xi_i \triangleq (\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}})/\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}} \ll L_i - 1$, the BLER in~\eqref{eq:bler_explicit} can be approximated as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx \frac{1}{(L_i-1)!} \left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}}\right)^{L_i-1} e^{-(\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}})/\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}},
\label{eq:bler_high_snr}
\end{equation}
for $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} > \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}$, and $\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx 0$ otherwise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The regularised incomplete gamma function admits the series expansion
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon(a, x) = \frac{x^a e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a)} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^k}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(a+j)}.
\label{eq:gamma_series}
\end{equation}
For $x \ll a$, the series is dominated by its first term ($k=0$), yielding
\begin{equation}
\Upsilon(a, x) \approx \frac{x^a e^{-x}}{a \cdot \Gamma(a)} = \frac{x^a e^{-x}}{\Gamma(a+1)}.
\label{eq:gamma_first_term}
\end{equation}
Substituting $a = L_i - 1$ and $x = (\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \gamma_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}})/\bar{\gamma}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}$ into~\eqref{eq:gamma_first_term} and using $\Gamma(L_i) = (L_i-1)!$ yields~\eqref{eq:bler_high_snr}.
\end{IEEEproof}

The approximation in Proposition~\ref{prop:bler_high_snr} reveals several important characteristics. First, the BLER scales as $\xi_i^{L_i-1}$, confirming that the system achieves a diversity order of $L_i - 1$ from the NLOS paths, with the LOS path providing a deterministic SNR floor. Second, the exponential factor $e^{-\xi_i}$ provides additional attenuation that becomes significant as the operating point moves further into the high-SNR regime. Third, the factorial term $(L_i-1)!$ in the denominator quantifies the coding gain achieved through MRC diversity combining.

For the special case of pure Rayleigh fading where $K_{i,0} = 0$ (i.e., all $L_i$ paths are NLOS), the deterministic LOS component is absent, and all $L_i$ paths contribute to the fading. Assuming equal average power $\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}} = \bar{\gamma}_{i,SU}/L_i$ per path, the combined SNR follows a gamma distribution with shape $L_i$ and scale $\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}}$, yielding
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx \Upsilon\left(L_i, \frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}}}\right) = 1 - e^{-\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}/\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}}} \sum_{m=0}^{L_i-1} \frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}}}\right)^m.
\label{eq:bler_rayleigh}
\end{equation}
In the high-SNR regime, this further simplifies to
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^{\mathrm{E2E}} \approx \frac{1}{L_i!} \left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}{\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}}}\right)^{L_i} e^{-\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}/\bar{\gamma}_{i}^{\mathrm{br}}},
\label{eq:bler_rayleigh_high_snr}
\end{equation}
which exhibits full diversity order $L_i$.%==============================================================================
\subsection{Ephemeris-Driven Outage Prediction}
\label{sec:ephemeris_prediction}

Building upon the finite-blocklength BLER analysis, this subsection develops an ephemeris-driven prediction framework that exploits the deterministic orbital mechanics of LEO satellites to estimate future channel states and compute reliability scores for each subchannel.

In rapidly time-varying LEO NTN deployments, the reliability of each downlink subchannel is predominantly governed by deterministic geometric factors, including satellite orbital motion, slant-range evolution as characterised by~\eqref{eq:slant_range}, elevation angle dynamics, and the resulting large-scale attenuation specified in~\eqref{eq:total_path_loss}. These geometric quantities can be accurately predicted using satellite ephemeris data, enabling proactive resource allocation decisions.

Let $t_k$ denote the start time of the $k$th OTFS frame. Decisions made at frame $k$ target a future frame indexed by $k + \Delta$, with $t_{k+\Delta} \approx t_k + T_{\Delta}$, where $T_{\Delta}$ denotes the total system latency. Using the predicted satellite position derived from ephemeris data and the estimated UE location, the predictor computes the future elevation angle $\tilde{\epsilon}_i[k+\Delta]$ and slant range $\tilde{d}_{i,k\ell}[k+\Delta]$.

The predicted large-scale path loss is computed by evaluating each component of the path loss model at the predicted geometry:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathrm{PL}}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{tot}}[k+\Delta] = \widetilde{\mathrm{PL}}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{FS}}[k+\Delta] + \tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{atm}}[k+\Delta] + \tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{i,k\ell}^{\mathrm{blk}}[k+\Delta],
\label{eq:predicted_path_loss}
\end{equation}
yielding the predicted large-scale gain $\tilde{G}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}[k+\Delta] = 10^{-\widetilde{\mathrm{PL}}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{tot}}[k+\Delta]/10}$. The predicted average SNR for the $k$--$\ell$ link is
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,k\ell}[k+\Delta] = \frac{P_k\, \mathcal{G}_k\, \tilde{G}_{i,k\ell,\mathrm{LS}}[k+\Delta]\, \mathcal{G}_\ell}{\sigma_{n,\ell}^2},
\label{eq:predicted_avg_snr}
\end{equation}
and the predicted end-to-end SNR follows from the cascaded relay model:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[k+\Delta] = \frac{\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{GS}}[k+\Delta] \cdot \tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{SU}}[k+\Delta]}{\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{GS}}[k+\Delta] + \tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{SU}}[k+\Delta] + 1}.
\label{eq:predicted_cascaded_snr}
\end{equation}

The predicted BLER is obtained using the closed-form approximation from Proposition~\ref{prop:bler_approx}. Under the dominant LOS approximation, the predicted average LOS SNR is $\tilde{\gamma}_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}[k+\Delta]$ and the predicted average NLOS SNR per path is $\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}[k+\Delta]$, satisfying the power budget $\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[k+\Delta] = \tilde{\gamma}_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}[k+\Delta] + (L_i-1)\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}[k+\Delta]$. For computational efficiency in real-time prediction, the high-SNR approximation from Proposition~\ref{prop:bler_high_snr} can be employed:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[k+\Delta] \approx \frac{1}{(L_i-1)!} &\left(\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \tilde{\gamma}_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}[k+\Delta]}{\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}[k+\Delta]}\right)^{L_i-1} \\
&\times e^{-\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} - \tilde{\gamma}_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}[k+\Delta]}{\tilde{\bar{\gamma}}_{i,\mathrm{NLOS}}[k+\Delta]}}
\end{split}
\label{eq:predicted_bler}
\end{equation}
for $\gamma_{\mathrm{th}} > \tilde{\gamma}_{i,0}^{\mathrm{det}}[k+\Delta]$, and $\tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[k+\Delta] \approx 0$ otherwise.

The reliability score for subchannel~$i$ is defined as
\begin{equation}
\eta_i[k+\Delta] = 1 - \tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[k+\Delta].
\label{eq:reliability_score}
\end{equation}
These reliability score are forwarded to the diversity transform layer, where they drive both the baseline transform design and the adaptive transform selection algorithm described in Section~\ref{sec:diversity_transform}.

\section{Diversity Transform Layer}
\label{sec:diversity_transform}
The diversity transform layer introduces controlled redundancy before the encoded data is distributed over multiple NTN downlink subchannels. A binary source message $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{F}_2^K$ is first partitioned into $\varrho$ independent encoder subwords using a non-trivial index partition
\[
\mathcal{B} = (B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_\varrho), \qquad \bigcup_{i=1}^\varrho B_i = \{1,\ldots,K\}.
\]
Each partition $B_i$ is independently FEC encoded to produce parallel subwords $\mathbf{x}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_\varrho \in \mathbb{F}_2^{n_{\mathrm{c}}}$. These represent $\varrho$ protected data flows that are subsequently mixed for diversity enhancement.

The diversity mapper linearly combines the $\varrho$ subwords using a rectangular binary transform matrix
\[
G_{\text{DIV}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}, \qquad n_{\text{out}} > \varrho,
\]
which introduces additional redundancy by expanding $\varrho$ encoded subwords into $n_{\text{out}}$ transform outputs. For each symbol position $\ell$ within the subwords,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}^{\ell} = G_{\text{DIV}}^{\mathsf{T}} \mathbf{x}^{\ell},
\end{equation}
and collecting all positions yields
\[
\mathbf{Z} = G_{\text{DIV}}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{X},
\]
where $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\varrho\times n_c}$ contains the input subwords and $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{n_{\text{out}}\times n_c}$ contains the transformed outputs. In this way, every part of the original message is spread across multiple transform outputs, improving recoverability under link failures.

The $n_{\text{out}}$ transformed subwords are assigned to $n$ physical subchannels via a fixed disjoint mapping
\[
\mathcal{A} = (A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_n), \qquad \bigcup_{i=1}^n A_i = \{1,\ldots,n_{\text{out}}\}.
\]
When subchannel $i$ experiences deep fading or blockage, all transform outputs in $A_i$ are erased, leading to a transform-domain erasure pattern
\begin{equation}
E = \bigcup_{i\in S} A_i ,
\end{equation}
where $S$ denotes the outage subchannel set. To guarantee that all data subwords remain recoverable under such losses, the diversity transform must satisfy the full-rank condition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fr_criterion_descriptive}
\mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\text{DIV}}(E^{c})\right)=\varrho, \qquad \forall\,E \in \mathcal{E},
\end{equation}
ensuring complete reconstruction for every outage pattern $E$ belonging to the predicted erasure family $\mathcal{E}$. Based on $\eta_i[k+\Delta]$, subchannels are classified into two disjoint sets:
\begin{IEEEeqnarray}{rCl}
S_{\mathrm{safe}}[k+\Delta] &=& \{i : \eta_i[k+\Delta] > \tau_{\mathrm{safe}}\}, \label{eq:safe_set}\\
S_{\mathrm{uncertain}}[k+\Delta] &=& \{i : \eta_i[k+\Delta] \le \tau_{\mathrm{safe}}\}, \label{eq:uncertain_set}
\end{IEEEeqnarray}
where $\tau_{\mathrm{safe}} \in (0,1)$ is a design threshold controlling the trade-off between robustness and spectral efficiency. Since uncertain subchannels may rapidly degrade due to blockage or rain bursts, subchannels predicted to be in outage ($\tilde{\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathrm{E2E}}[k+\Delta] > \epsilon_{\mathrm{th}}$) are modelled as block erasures in the diversity-transform domain, with the robust outage family
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{robust}}[k+\Delta] = \left\{ \bigcup_{i\in \mathcal{S}} \mathcal{A}_i : 
\mathcal{S} \subseteq S_{\mathrm{uncertain}}[k+\Delta] \right\},
\label{eq:erasure_pattern}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_i$ denotes the set of transform-output indices assigned to subchannel~$i$.

A baseline transform matrix $G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\varrho\times n_{\text{out}}}$ is constructed to guarantee recovery under the maximum number of subchannel failures the system must tolerate. Let $f_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{sys}}$ denote this maximum number of simultaneous subchannel failures, an absolute upper bound determined by operational requirements. The transform is designed such that:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c})\right)=\varrho, 
\qquad \forall\, E \text{ with } |\{i : A_i \subseteq E\}| \leq f_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{sys}},
\end{equation}
This guarantees complete recovery under any failure pattern involving up to $f_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{sys}}$ subchannels, whether predicted or not. The robust outage family $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{robust}}[k+\Delta]$ defined in~\eqref{eq:erasure_pattern} is a subset of these guaranteed recoverable patterns, meaning the baseline provides a certified protection floor that extends beyond predicted scenarios to handle unexpected outages. The algebraic construction of $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ from MDS codes is detailed in Section~\ref{sec:diversity_transform_construction}, and the adaptive transform family that exploits predicted subchannel reliability while strictly preserving these outage-recovery guarantees is formally developed in Section~\ref{subsec:adaptive_transform}.

\subsection{Construction of Diversity Transform Matrices}
\label{sec:diversity_transform_construction}
The full-rank recovery criterion is realised through generator matrices of
maximum distance separable (MDS) codes, specifically Reed--Solomon  codes, defined over binary extension fields~\cite{Seroussi1986}. A faithful
field-to-binary representation maps each field element to a square binary
matrix, yielding a block-structured diversity transform that aligns with the
block-erasure behaviour of LEO satellite outages, where the loss of a single
satellite erases an entire block of transform outputs. The MDS singleton bound ensures that any set of surviving blocks whose cardinality meets or exceeds the code dimension preserves full rank, providing the maximum possible outage protection for a given redundancy level \cite{Roth2006,HuffmanPless2003}. An
$[n_{\mathrm{s}},k_{\mathrm{c}},d_{\min}]_{\mathfrak{q}}$ MDS code with
$d_{\min} = n_{\mathrm{s}}-k_{\mathrm{c}}+1$ ensures that any set of
$k_{\mathrm{c}}$ field-symbol columns of its generator matrix is linearly
independent over $\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}$, where $\mathfrak{q} = 2^{m}$,
with the formal recovery guarantee established in Theorem~\ref{thm:block_mds}.

Let $G_{\mathrm{MDS}} \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k_{\mathrm{c}} \times n_{\mathrm{s}}}$ be the generator matrix
of the selected MDS code. The objective is to construct from $G_{\mathrm{MDS}}$
a binary diversity transform $G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$
with $\varrho = k_{\mathrm{c}} m$ and $n_{\text{out}} = n_{\mathrm{s}} m$, while preserving the field-symbol independence
structure of the underlying MDS code. To achieve this, a mapping
\[
\varphi : \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}} \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_{2}^{m \times m}
\]
is used, where $\varphi$ is a faithful matrix representation satisfying
\[
\varphi(a+b)=\varphi(a)+\varphi(b),\qquad \varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b),
\]
for all $a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}$, and is injective. For binary extension fields
$\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}$, faithful representations always exist. A standard choice
is the regular (companion-matrix) representation, where $\varphi(g)$ describes
binary multiplication by the field element~$g$.

Applying $\varphi$ to every symbol of $G_{\mathrm{MDS}}$ produces a block matrix
\[
G_{\mathrm{FIX}}^{\mathrm{block}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\varphi(g_{1,1}) & \varphi(g_{1,2}) & \cdots & \varphi(g_{1,n_{\mathrm{s}}}) \\
\varphi(g_{2,1}) & \varphi(g_{2,2}) & \cdots & \varphi(g_{2,n_{\mathrm{s}}}) \\
\vdots        & \vdots        & \ddots & \vdots        \\
\varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},1}) & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},2}) & \cdots & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},n_{\mathrm{s}}})
\end{bmatrix},
\]
whose entries are full $m \times m$ binary matrices. Because $\varphi$ preserves
field addition and multiplication and is injective, the linear-independence
guarantees of the underlying MDS code are preserved at the block-column level
of $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}^{\mathrm{block}}$.

Flattening each $m \times m$ block into $m$ consecutive rows yields the final
binary transform matrix
\[
G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}, \qquad \varrho = k_{\mathrm{c}} m,\; n_{\text{out}} = n_{\mathrm{s}} m.
\]

The flattened matrix has the structure:
\[
G_{\mathrm{FIX}} = 
\begin{bmatrix}
\varphi(g_{1,1})_1 & \varphi(g_{1,2})_1 & \cdots & \varphi(g_{1,n_{\mathrm{s}}})_1 \\
\varphi(g_{1,1})_2 & \varphi(g_{1,2})_2 & \cdots & \varphi(g_{1,n_{\mathrm{s}}})_2 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\varphi(g_{1,1})_m & \varphi(g_{1,2})_m & \cdots & \varphi(g_{1,n_{\mathrm{s}}})_m \\
\varphi(g_{2,1})_1 & \varphi(g_{2,2})_1 & \cdots & \varphi(g_{2,n_{\mathrm{s}}})_1 \\
\varphi(g_{2,1})_2 & \varphi(g_{2,2})_2 & \cdots & \varphi(g_{2,n_{\mathrm{s}}})_2 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\varphi(g_{2,1})_m & \varphi(g_{2,2})_m & \cdots & \varphi(g_{2,n_{\mathrm{s}}})_m \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},1})_1 & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},2})_1 & \cdots & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},n_{\mathrm{s}}})_1 \\
\varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},1})_2 & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},2})_2 & \cdots & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},n_{\mathrm{s}}})_2 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},1})_m & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},2})_m & \cdots & \varphi(g_{k_{\mathrm{c}},n_{\mathrm{s}}})_m
\end{bmatrix}
\]
where $\varphi(g_{i,j})_\ell$ denotes the $\ell$-th row (a $1 \times m$ binary row vector) of the $m \times m$ binary matrix $\varphi(g_{i,j})$.

Each field symbol $g_{i,j} \in \mathbb{F}_{2^m}$ produces a group of $m$
adjacent binary columns, forming the field-symbol blocks
\[
\mathcal{C}_j = \{(j-1)m+1,\ldots,jm\}, \qquad j = 1,\ldots,n_{\mathrm{s}}.
\]
Erasures are treated at the level of these blocks, ensuring consistency with the structure imposed by the binary lifting. To ensure that physical subchannel outages induce precisely block-level erasures, we set the number of physical subchannels equal to the number of field-symbol blocks, i.e., $n = n_{\mathrm{s}}$, and assign the entire block $\mathcal{C}_j$ to physical subchannel $j$. The MDS property guarantees full row-rank recovery under any pattern of block-aligned erasures. Let $E \subseteq \{1,\ldots,n_{\text{out}}\}$ be an erasure pattern such that each block is either fully retained or fully erased:
\[
\mathcal{C}_j \cap E = \emptyset \quad \text{or} \quad \mathcal{C}_j \subseteq E .
\]
Let the surviving blocks be
\[
\mathcal{J}(E) = \{ j \in \{1,\ldots,n_{\mathrm{s}}\} : \mathcal{C}_j \cap E = \emptyset \}.
\]
If at least $k_{\mathrm{c}}$ blocks survive, then the remaining columns preserve full rank:

\begin{theorem}[MDS Guarantees Under Block-Aligned Erasures]
\label{thm:block_mds}
Let $G_{\mathrm{MDS}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2^m}^{k_{\mathrm{c}} \times n_{\mathrm{s}}}$ be the generator matrix of an MDS code and let $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ be its binary lifting under a faithful representation $\varphi$. If at least $k_{\mathrm{c}}$ field-symbol blocks survive, i.e.,
\[
|\mathcal{J}(E)| \geq k_{\mathrm{c}},
\]
then the surviving binary submatrix has full row rank,
\[
\operatorname{rank}\bigl(G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^c)\bigr)=k_{\mathrm{c}} m,
\]
and all $k_{\mathrm{c}} m$ encoded subwords are perfectly recoverable from the surviving transform outputs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{IEEEproof}
The surviving columns indexed by $\mathcal{J}(E)$ correspond to the restricted linear map
\[
T_{\mathcal{J}(E)} : \mathbb{F}_{2^m}^{k_{\mathrm{c}}} \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_{2^m}^{|\mathcal{J}(E)|}.
\]
Because $G_{\mathrm{MDS}}$ is MDS, any $k_{\mathrm{c}}$ columns are linearly independent; hence $T_{\mathcal{J}(E)}$ is injective whenever $|\mathcal{J}(E)| \ge k_{\mathrm{c}}$.

Applying the faithful representation $\varphi$ produces an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear map
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal{J}(E)} : \mathbb{F}_2^{k_{\mathrm{c}} m} \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_2^{m|\mathcal{J}(E)|},
\]
whose matrix is exactly $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^c)$. Since $\varphi$ is injective and multiplicative, it preserves the injectivity of $T_{\mathcal{J}(E)}$. Thus, $\Phi_{\mathcal{J}(E)}$ has a trivial kernel and full rank $k_{\mathrm{c}} m$, yielding the stated result.
\end{IEEEproof}

\subsubsection*{Reed--Solomon Construction}

For the explicit instantiation of the MDS generator matrix $G_{\mathrm{MDS}}$,
we employ a Reed--Solomon (RS) construction over $\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}$. Let
$\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{\mathrm{s}}}$ be $n_{\mathrm{s}}$ distinct nonzero elements of
$\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}$. The RS generator matrix is the Vandermonde matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{G}^{\star(k_{\mathrm{c}})} =
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\
\alpha_1 & \alpha_2 & \cdots & \alpha_{n_{\mathrm{s}}} \\
\alpha_1^2 & \alpha_2^2 & \cdots & \alpha_{n_{\mathrm{s}}}^2 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\alpha_1^{k_{\mathrm{c}}-1} & \alpha_2^{k_{\mathrm{c}}-1} & \cdots & \alpha_{n_{\mathrm{s}}}^{k_{\mathrm{c}}-1}
\end{bmatrix}
\in \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}^{k_{\mathrm{c}} \times n_{\mathrm{s}}}.
\label{eq:RS_generator_final}
\end{equation}

Because the evaluation points are distinct, every $k_{\mathrm{c}} \times k_{\mathrm{c}}$ submatrix of
$\mathbf{G}^{\star(k_{\mathrm{c}})}$ is nonsingular. Thus, any $k_{\mathrm{c}}$ columns of
$\mathbf{G}^{\star(k_{\mathrm{c}})}$ are linearly independent, and the RS code achieves the
MDS property with minimum distance $d_{\min} = n_{\mathrm{s}}-k_{\mathrm{c}}+1$.

When $\mathbf{G}^{\star(k_{\mathrm{c}})}$ is passed through the faithful representation
$\varphi(\cdot)$, each field symbol $\alpha_j^{\ell}$ is replaced by an
$m \times m$ binary matrix $\varphi(\alpha_j^{\ell})$. This produces the block
matrix $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}^{\mathrm{block}}$, and after flattening each block into
$m$ rows, the final binary diversity transform
\[
G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{k_{\mathrm{c}} m \times n_{\mathrm{s}} m}
\]
is obtained. The MDS property of the RS matrix ensures that any $k_{\mathrm{c}}$ surviving
field-symbol blocks preserve full rank, guaranteeing
\[
\mathrm{rank}\bigl(G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c})\bigr) = k_{\mathrm{c}} m
\quad\text{whenever } |\mathcal{J}(E)| \ge k_{\mathrm{c}}.
\]

The RS construction therefore provides a compact, algebraically robust, and
dimension-flexible method for generating $G_{\mathrm{MDS}}$ while preserving all
necessary independence guarantees required by the diversity transform.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Construction of Fixed Diversity Transform Matrix from MDS Codes}
\label{alg:div_transform_construction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Desired dimensions $\varrho$ (number of encoder outputs) and $n_{\text{out}}$ (number of diversity transform outputs) with $n_{\text{out}} > \varrho \geq 2$
\Ensure Binary diversity transform matrix $G_{\text{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$
\Statex
\Procedure{Construct Fixed Diversity Transform }{$\varrho$, $n_{\text{out}}$}
    \State \textbf{Step 1: Parameter Selection}
    \State Find integers $m, k_{\mathrm{c}}, n_{\mathrm{s}}$ such that:
    \State \quad $\varrho = k_{\mathrm{c}} m$ and $n_{\text{out}} = n_{\mathrm{s}} m$
    \State \quad $n_{\mathrm{s}} > k_{\mathrm{c}} \geq 1$, $m \geq 1$
    \State Select a finite field $\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}$ of size $\mathfrak{q} = 2^{m}$
    \State \textbf{Step 2: MDS Code Selection}
    \State Choose an $[n_{\mathrm{s}}, k_{\mathrm{c}}, d_{\min}]_{\mathfrak{q}}$ MDS code with $d_{\min} = n_{\mathrm{s}}-k_{\mathrm{c}}+1$
    \State Let $G_{\text{MDS}} \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{k_{\mathrm{c}} \times n_{\mathrm{s}}}$ be its generator matrix
    \State \textbf{Step 3: Faithful Representation}
    \State Define $\varphi: \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}} \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_{2}^{m \times m}$ such that:
    \State \quad $\varphi(a + b) = \varphi(a) + \varphi(b)$ for all $a,b \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}$
    \State \quad $\varphi(ab) = \varphi(a)\varphi(b)$ for all $a,b \in \mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{q}}$
    \State \quad $\varphi$ is injective
    \State \textbf{Step 4: Block Matrix Formation}
    \State Initialise a $k_{\mathrm{c}} \times n_{\mathrm{s}}$ block matrix $G_{\text{FIX}}^{\mathrm{block}}$ whose entries are $m \times m$ binary matrices
    \For{$i = 1$ \textbf{to} $k_{\mathrm{c}}$}
        \For{$j = 1$ \textbf{to} $n_{\mathrm{s}}$}
            \State $G_{\text{FIX}}^{\mathrm{block}}[i,j] \gets \varphi\bigl(G_{\text{MDS}}[i,j]\bigr)$
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{Step 5: Matrix Flattening}
    \State Initialise $G_{\text{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$ as the all-zero matrix
    \For{$i = 1$ \textbf{to} $k_{\mathrm{c}}$}
        \For{$j = 1$ \textbf{to} $n_{\mathrm{s}}$}
            \For{$\ell = 1$ \textbf{to} $m$}
                \State $row\_index \gets (i-1)m + \ell$
                \State $col\_start \gets (j-1)m + 1$
                \State $col\_end \gets j m$
                \State Set row $row\_index$ and columns $col\_start{:}col\_end$ of $G_{\text{FIX}}$ equal to the $\ell$-th row of $G_{\text{FIX}}^{\mathrm{block}}[i,j]$
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $G_{\text{FIX}}$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Adaptive diversity transforms}
\label{subsec:adaptive_transform}

We now formalise the theoretical mechanism used to construct the adaptive
diversity transforms employed in the proposed hybrid architecture. The key
design principle is that adaptation must not compromise the full-rank outage
recovery guarantee established in Theorem~\ref{thm:block_mds} for the baseline
transform $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$.

Let $G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$ denote a fixed binary
diversity transform that satisfies the full-rank condition for a prescribed
family $\mathcal{E}$ of transform-output erasure patterns:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fullrank_fix_family}
\mathrm{rank}\!\bigl(G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^c)\bigr) = \varrho,
\qquad \forall\, E \in \mathcal{E},
\end{equation}
where $E^c$ denotes the set of surviving transform-output indices.

Adaptive diversity transforms are obtained by left-multiplying
$G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ with invertible matrices over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Specifically,
we define the adaptive family
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adaptive_family}
G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)} = T^{(\iota)} G_{\mathrm{FIX}},
\qquad \iota = 0,1,\ldots,L_{\mathrm{a}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
T^{(\iota)} \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2),
\qquad T^{(0)} = I_\varrho .
\end{equation}
Each matrix $T^{(\iota)}$ is invertible and can be realised as a finite product
of elementary row operations (row permutations and binary row additions), all of
which preserve linear independence over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

Since adaptation is realised exclusively through invertible row mixing, every
adaptive transform $G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)}$ is generated directly from
$G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ and inherits its outage-recovery guarantees. The role of
adaptation is therefore not to alter the set of recoverable erasure patterns,
but to redistribute how the $\varrho$ encoder subwords participate in the
$n_{\text{out}}$ transform outputs under a fixed physical assignment.

The following theorem establishes that the full-rank condition in
\eqref{eq:fullrank_fix_family} is preserved for all adaptive transforms of the
form \eqref{eq:adaptive_family}.

\begin{theorem}[Rank Preservation Under Adaptive Left Transforms]
\label{thm:adaptive_rank_preserve}
Let $G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$ be a
diversity--transform matrix that satisfies the full-rank outage criterion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fullrank_FIX}
\mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c})\right)=\varrho,
\qquad \forall\,E\in\mathcal{E},
\end{equation}
where $E^{c}$ denotes the surviving column set associated with the erasure
pattern $E$, and $\mathcal{E}$ is the family of transform-output erasure
patterns dictated by subchannel outages.

Let $T \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)$ be any invertible $\varrho \times \varrho$
matrix over $\mathbb{F}_{2}$, and define the corresponding adaptive diversity
transform
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{DIV}} = T\,G_{\mathrm{FIX}}.
\end{equation}
Then the adaptive transform preserves the outage guarantees of the baseline
transform, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rank_preserve_DIV}
\mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(E^{c})\right)=\varrho,
\qquad \forall\,E\in\mathcal{E}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider any erasure pattern $E \in \mathcal{E}$, and let $E^{c}$ denote its
surviving transform-output index set. The corresponding surviving submatrix of
the adaptive transform is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:submatrix_survival}
G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(E^{c})
= \bigl(T\,G_{\mathrm{FIX}}\bigr)(E^{c})
= T \, G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c}),
\end{equation}
where the last equality follows because deletion of columns affects only column
indices and does not interfere with left multiplication.

Since $T \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)$, it is invertible and can therefore be
written as a finite product of elementary row operations over $\mathbb{F}_{2}$
(row permutations and binary row additions). Such operations do not alter linear
dependence or independence relations among the columns of any matrix they act
upon. Consequently, applying $T$ on the left cannot reduce the column rank of
$G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c})$. Formally,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rank_preservation_step}
\mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(E^{c})\right)
= \mathrm{rank}\!\left(T \, G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c})\right)
= \mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{FIX}}(E^{c})\right).
\end{equation}
Substituting the baseline full-rank condition \eqref{eq:fullrank_FIX} yields
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rank}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(E^{c})\right)=\varrho,
\qquad \forall\,E\in\mathcal{E},
\end{equation}
which proves that every adaptive transform $G_{\mathrm{DIV}} = T\,G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$
inherits the same outage-decodability guarantees as $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$.
\end{proof}

The adaptive mechanism operates as follows: the ephemeris-driven predictor
selects an index $\iota^{\star}$ from a finite set, and the corresponding
invertible transform $T^{(\iota^{\star})}$ remixes the $\varrho$ encoder
subwords before they are mapped to $n_{\text{out}}$ outputs.
Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive_rank_preserve} guarantees that any such remixing
preserves the decodability of all erasure patterns in $\mathcal{E}$. The
benefit of adaptation is not to enlarge $\mathcal{E}$ but to bias the
redundancy allocation in favour of subchannels predicted to be reliable,
thereby reducing the error probability under actual random channel realisations
while maintaining the same worst-case outage protection.

\begin{theorem}[Performance Advantage of Adaptive Transform Construction]
\label{thm:adaptive_perf_gain}
Let $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ be a baseline diversity transform satisfying the
full-rank outage recovery condition over the erasure family $\mathcal{E}$. Let
$E \in \mathcal{E}$ denote the random transform-output erasure pattern, and let
$S$ denote side information available at the transmitter (e.g.,
ephemeris-based reliability predictions).

Consider any adaptive selection policy $\pi$ that maps side information $S$ to
an invertible row-mixing matrix $T(S) \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)$, yielding
the adaptive transform
\[
G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(S) = T(S)\,G_{\mathrm{FIX}} .
\]
Let $P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(G,E\right)$ denote the frame-error probability when
transform $G$ is used and erasure pattern $E$ occurs. Define
\begin{align}
P_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{fix}}
&\triangleq \mathbb{E}_{E}\!\left[
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{FIX}},E\right)
\right], \\[4pt]
P_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{adapt}}
&\triangleq \mathbb{E}_{S,E}\!\left[
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(S),E\right)
\right].
\end{align}
If the policy $\pi$ is conditionally optimal, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:policy_optimality}
T(S) \in \arg\min_{T \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)}
\mathbb{E}_{E \mid S}\!\left[
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(T G_{\mathrm{FIX}},E\right)
\right]
\quad \text{a.s.},
\end{equation}
then the average frame-error probability satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adaptive_dominates}
P_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{adapt}}
\leq
P_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{fix}} .
\end{equation}
Moreover, if the side information $S$ is statistically informative about $E$
and there exist invertible transforms $T_1,T_2 \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)$
such that
\[
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(T_1 G_{\mathrm{FIX}},E\right)
\neq
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(T_2 G_{\mathrm{FIX}},E\right)
\quad \text{for some } E \in \mathcal{E},
\]
then the inequality in \eqref{eq:adaptive_dominates} is strict.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any realisation $s$ of the side information $S$, define the conditional
error cost
\begin{equation}
J(T \mid s)
\triangleq
\mathbb{E}_{E \mid S=s}\!\left[
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(T G_{\mathrm{FIX}},E\right)
\right],
\end{equation}
for $T \in \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)$. By the conditional optimality of policy
$\pi$ in \eqref{eq:policy_optimality},
\begin{equation}
J(T(S) \mid s)
\leq
J(I_\varrho \mid s),
\quad \forall s \in \mathcal{S},
\end{equation}
where $I_\varrho$ corresponds to the baseline transform $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$.
Taking expectation over $S$ and applying the law of iterated expectations
yields
\begin{align}
P_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{adapt}}
&= \mathbb{E}_{S}\!\left[ J(T(S) \mid S) \right] \\
&\leq \mathbb{E}_{S}\!\left[ J(I_\varrho \mid S) \right] \\
&= \mathbb{E}_{E}\!\left[
P_{\mathrm{e}}\!\left(G_{\mathrm{FIX}},E\right)
\right]
= P_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{fix}},
\end{align}
which proves \eqref{eq:adaptive_dominates}. For strict inequality, if $S$ is
informative about $E$ and different invertible row-mixing matrices induce
distinct error probabilities for some erasure patterns, then on a set of $s$
with positive probability the selected transform $T(S)$ strictly improves the
conditional error cost, yielding a strict inequality after averaging.
\end{proof}

Since the physical assignment of transform outputs to subchannels is fixed by
$\mathcal{A} = (A_1,\ldots,A_n)$, left multiplication by $T^{(\iota)}$
redistributes which encoder subwords participate in the linear combinations
conveyed by each transform output. To quantify how effectively encoded
information is placed on reliable subchannels, the reliability-weighted quality
metric is defined as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quality_metric}
Q^{(\iota)} = \sum_{i \in S_{\mathrm{safe}}[k+\Delta]} \eta_i[k+\Delta]
\sum_{j \in A_i} \left\| G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)}(:,j) \right\|_0,
\end{equation}
where $\eta_i[k+\Delta]$ is the predicted reliability of subchannel~$i$ and
$\|G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)}(:,j)\|_0$ counts the number of encoder subwords
participating in the $j$th transform output. The optimal adaptive transform is
then selected as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:optimal_selection}
G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{\star}[k+\Delta] =
\arg\max_{G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)} \in \mathcal{G}}\; Q^{(\iota)},
\end{equation}
concentrating encoded information on subchannels predicted to be reliable
while the optimisation is restricted to the rank-preserving family
$\mathcal{G}$, so that the worst-case outage recovery guarantees of
$G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ remain intact. The full selection procedure is formalised in Algorithm~\ref{alg:adaptive_transform_selection}.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Adaptive Diversity Transform Selection}
\label{alg:adaptive_transform_selection}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Baseline transform $G_{\mathrm{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$
satisfying the full-rank criterion over $\mathcal{E}$;
fixed output-to-subchannel assignment $\mathcal{A} = (A_1,\ldots,A_n)$;
finite set of invertible row-mixing matrices
$\mathcal{T} = \{T^{(0)},\ldots,T^{(L_{\mathrm{a}})}\} \subset \mathrm{GL}(\varrho,2)$
with $T^{(0)} = I_\varrho$;
predicted subchannel reliabilities $\{\eta_i[k+\Delta]\}_{i=1}^{n}$;
reliable subchannel set $S_{\mathrm{safe}}[k+\Delta]$
\Ensure Selected adaptive diversity transform $G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^\star$ and index $\iota^\star$
\Statex
\Procedure{SelectAdaptiveTransform}{$G_{\mathrm{FIX}}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T},
\{\eta_i[k+\Delta]\}, S_{\mathrm{safe}}[k+\Delta]$}
    \For{$\iota = 0$ \textbf{to} $L_{\mathrm{a}}$}
        \State $G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)} \gets T^{(\iota)} G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$
        \State $Q^{(\iota)} \gets 0$
        \For{each $i \in S_{\mathrm{safe}}[k+\Delta]$}
            \State $\omega_i^{(\iota)} \gets
            \sum_{j \in A_i} \left\| G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{(\iota)}(:,j) \right\|_0$
            \Comment{Sum of column sparsities}
            \State $Q^{(\iota)} \gets Q^{(\iota)} + \eta_i[k+\Delta] \cdot \omega_i^{(\iota)}$
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \State $\iota^\star \gets \arg\max_{\iota \in \{0,\ldots,L_{\mathrm{a}}\}} Q^{(\iota)}$
    \State $G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^\star \gets T^{(\iota^\star)} G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$
    \State \Return $G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^\star$, $\iota^\star$
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\subsection{Error Correction Code with Diversity Transform Integration}
\label{subsec:polar_diversity}
\label{subsec:polar_diversity}
Polar codes are adopted as the FEC component owing to their capacity-achieving performance and compatibility with the iterative demapping architecture. The
diversity transform operates as an inner outage-protection stage, while the
polar code serves as the outer error-correction component. Let $G_{\text{POL}}$ denote the generator matrix of an $(N,K)$ polar code, and
let $G_{\text{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$ be the baseline diversity
transform constructed in Section~\ref{sec:diversity_transform_construction}.
For a polar input matrix $U$, the combined encoding operation is
\begin{equation}
    Z = G_{\text{FIX}}^{\mathsf{T}}\, U\, G_{\text{POL}},
\end{equation}
where $Z$ contains the $n_{\text{out}}$ transform outputs to be mapped onto the fixed set of
NTN subchannels. Vectorising both sides yields
\begin{equation}
    z = u\left(G_{\text{POL}} \otimes G_{\text{FIX}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $u$ and $z$ are the vectorised forms of $U$ and $Z$, and
$G_{\text{PD}} = G_{\text{POL}} \otimes G_{\text{FIX}}$ is the combined polar--diversity generator.
Since $G_{\text{FIX}}$ is rectangular with $n_{\text{out}} > \varrho$, the composite matrix
$G_{\text{PD}}$ is also rectangular, thereby embedding the desired redundancy
directly into the transform domain.

The mapping from the information word $\mathbf{d}$ to the polar input positions
is performed via a structured partition. Let
$\mathcal{B} = (B_1,\ldots,B_\varrho)$ be a non-trivial partition of the $K$ input
bits, and let $\mathcal{D} = (D_1,\ldots,D_\varrho)$ denote mutually disjoint index
sets of frozen and unfrozen polar positions arranged according to reliability
ordering. For each $i \in \{1,\ldots,\varrho\}$, the assignment rule
\begin{equation}
    (\mathbf{u}_i)_{D_i} = \mathbf{d}_{B_i}
\end{equation}
ensures that each encoder subword is individually protected by the polar code
and subsequently mixed across multiple diversity outputs.

This construction requires no bit-level interleaving. The redundancy is instead
generated through the algebraic structure of $G_{\text{FIX}}$ and preserved
through the Kronecker product. Consequently, the combined transform
$G_{\text{PD}}$ inherits the full-rank and rank-profile guarantees proved in
Sections~\textsc{II}--\textsc{IV}, enabling certified resilience against
subchannel outages while simultaneously leveraging polar-code reliability for
noise-limited performance.
\subsection{Receiver-Side Diversity Demapping}
\label{subsec:receiver_demapping}

The receiver performs diversity demapping to recover the $\varrho$ encoder
subwords from the transform outputs received across the $n$ physical
subchannels. The demapping procedure is applicable to both the fixed baseline
transform $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$ and the adaptive transform
$G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{\star} = T^{(\iota^\star)} G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$, and in both
cases the full-rank guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:block_mds} ensures that the
system of equations is solvable whenever the number of surviving subchannels
meets the MDS recovery threshold.

The received transform outputs from surviving subchannels form the linear
system
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z}(E^c) = G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{\mathsf{T}}(E^c)\,\mathbf{X},
\end{equation}
where $E^c$ denotes the set of surviving transform-output indices and
$G_{\mathrm{DIV}} \in \{G_{\mathrm{FIX}},\, G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{\star}\}$
denotes the transform in use. By Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive_rank_preserve},
whenever $|\mathcal{J}(E)| \geq k_{\mathrm{c}}$ the submatrix
$G_{\mathrm{DIV}}(E^c)$ has full row rank $\varrho$, and the encoder subword
matrix $\mathbf{X}$ is uniquely recoverable via the binary left inverse
$G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{\dagger}(E^c)$, which can be precomputed offline for all
anticipated erasure patterns in $\mathcal{E}$. When the adaptive transform is
used, the selected mixing matrix index $\iota^\star$ is signalled to the
receiver through the control channel, so that the correct left inverse is
applied without additional overhead.

For soft-decision decoding, the demapper employs an iterative procedure that
alternates between demapping and decoding steps. In each iteration, the
demapper computes soft log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) from channel outputs and
prior decision feedback from the FEC decoder; the decoder produces a partial
decision that is re-encoded and fed back to the demapper. Since the
transform-to-subchannel assignment $\mathcal{A}$ is fixed and known a priori
at both transmitter and receiver, reliability-aware LLR scaling is applied at
each iteration. For each transform output $j$ assigned to subchannel~$i$,
where $j \in A_i$, the LLR is first computed as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:llr_compute}
\Lambda_i^{(j)} = \ln \frac{\Pr(x_{i,j}=0 \mid \mathbf{y},
\hat{x}_1,\ldots,\hat{x}_{i-1})}{\Pr(x_{i,j}=1 \mid \mathbf{y},
\hat{x}_1,\ldots,\hat{x}_{i-1})},
\end{equation}
and then scaled by the instantaneous subchannel reliability score obtained
from the receiver's local channel estimate:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:llr_scaling}
\Lambda_i^{(j)} \leftarrow \hat{\eta}_i \cdot \Lambda_i^{(j)},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\eta}_i = 1 - \epsilon_i(\hat{\gamma}_i^{\mathrm{E2E}}, n_i, R)$
is evaluated from the receiver-estimated end-to-end SNR
$\hat{\gamma}_i^{\mathrm{E2E}}$ using the finite-blocklength error probability
of~\eqref{eq:ppv_approx}. Note that this instantaneous reliability estimate
requires no feedback to the transmitter: it is computed entirely at the
receiver from standard channel-estimation outputs. This scaling attenuates
potentially misleading soft information from unreliable subchannels while
amplifying contributions from reliable ones.
For the fixed transform, all subchannels are treated with equal structural
weight by $G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$, so the reliability scaling in
\eqref{eq:llr_scaling} provides the sole mechanism for exploiting the
heterogeneous subchannel quality at the receiver. For the adaptive transform,
the receiver-side reliability weighting is complementary to the transmitter-side
optimisation: $G_{\mathrm{DIV}}^{\star}$ has already concentrated encoded
information on predicted reliable subchannels via ephemeris-based transform
selection (Algorithm~\ref{alg:adaptive_transform_selection}), and the
instantaneous LLR scaling further reinforces this bias at the receiver using
up-to-date channel knowledge, jointly maximising decoding performance.
When the FEC encoder is a polar code, the successive cancellation list (SCL)
decoder provides naturally ordered partial decisions, making the iterative
architecture particularly efficient, as decoded bits are produced sequentially
in the order required by the demapper feedback loop. Each recovered subword
$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i$ is subsequently passed to the FEC decoder independently,
and the combined polar--diversity generator $G_{\mathrm{PD}} = G_{\mathrm{POL}}
\otimes G_{\mathrm{FIX}}$, detailed in
Section~\ref{subsec:polar_diversity}, preserves the algebraic rank guarantees
throughout the decoding process without requiring bit-level interleaving.
\subsection{Design Considerations}

Several practical considerations guide the selection of parameters and the design of the adaptive family:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Complexity:} The dimension $m$ of the representation affects the size of the binary transform $G_{\text{FIX}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\varrho \times n_{\text{out}}}$ and the cost of linear operations involving it, which typically scale with $O(\varrho n_{\text{out}}) = O(k_{\mathrm{c}} m \cdot n m)$. In practice, small values of $m$ (e.g., 2--4) are preferred to limit implementation complexity. The adaptive matrices $T^{(\iota)}$ are $\varrho \times \varrho$ and can be implemented as sparse products of elementary operations.
\end{itemize
